# ONA Healthy

A family farm in Phichai district, Uttaradit that grows pesticide-free tomatoes
in its own greenhouses and processes them on its own machinery on the same land.
Two product lines come out of one crop: things you drink or swallow, and things
you put on your skin. Trading for roughly eight to nine years — the sources
disagree, so check `references/business.md` before printing a number.

The single most important thing about this business, and the thing every
deliverable should carry: **nothing is outsourced.** Seed selection, nursery,
growing, cold pressing, soap curing, bottling and QC all happen in one place, by
one family. That is what competitors with contract manufacturers cannot copy, and
it is why "traceable to the harvest" is a factual claim here rather than a slogan.

Read the business file before writing any copy. Prices, award years and product
names are easy to get subtly wrong, and a brochure with an invented price is
worse than no brochure.

## Ground rules that apply to every deliverable

**Do not invent capability.** The farm's own documents flag several things as
unresolved: brand architecture between ONA Healthy / Onato / AMANA was never
decided, and production is still family-scale.
If a piece needs a price that does not exist, write "สอบถามราคา" rather than
guessing. If it needs a service the farm has never offered — workshops, tour
packages, shipping tiers — either leave it out or mark it clearly as a proposal.
This has come up repeatedly; the farm cannot deliver on promises made in print.

**Evidence beats adjectives.** This brand has real proof — FDA registration, the Q
mark, OTOP status, a Provincial Star award in 2562, 100% recommendation across its
reviews. Copy that leans on those lands far harder than copy that reaches for
"premium" or "the best". Prefer a dated fact or a certificate over an intensifier,
and prefer an award year over a count of years in business, which is unsettled.

**Red is a garnish, not a ground.** See `references/brand.md` for the reasoning,
but the short version: the farm's actual retail space is white end to end, with
tomatoes appearing as small red points in it. Large red fills read as a discount
sticker and cheapen the brand instantly.

**Thai is the primary language.** English is a real second language for the site,
not an afterthought — but Thai is what the business runs in. When producing print,
ask which language the piece is for rather than assuming.

## Open questions worth raising

If a task touches these, it is worth flagging to the user rather than quietly
picking a side — they are business decisions the owner has not made yet:

- **Brand architecture.** Is ONA Healthy the only name, with Onato and AMANA as
  product lines under it? Or are they separate brands? Retailers and new customers
  currently cannot tell, and renaming after the brand grows is expensive.
- **The serum's identity.** The brochure renamed it and described a different
  formulation from every earlier source. The near-identical juice entry turned out
  to be a whole separate product, so assume nothing here either —
  `references/business.md` sets it out. Ask rather than guess.
- **Photography.** There are no product or farm photographs in this repo. Vector
  illustration has been used as a stand-in on the web; for print it will not hold
  up. Real photography is the single highest-leverage thing this brand is missing.
